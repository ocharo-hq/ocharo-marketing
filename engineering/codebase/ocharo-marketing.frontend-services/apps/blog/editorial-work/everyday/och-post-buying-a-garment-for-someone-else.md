# Buying a garment for someone else?

*Last updated: 2026-09-27 12:07 AM*

## Editorial draft

The surprise can survive a useful question. Separate what you know about the person from what you are guessing.

**Reader promise:** Choose a clothing gift around the recipient's stated preferences and the information still missing.

**Format:** Gift decision guide

## Manuscript

A clothing gift has two points of view: the person choosing it and the person who will wear it. Suppose you know a favourite colour but not the recipient's preferred length, fastening, or looseness. Those blanks can make a beautiful choice difficult to wear. The surprise can survive a useful question. You can leave something delightful to discover while agreeing on the part that needs to fit.

### Separate liking from using

Someone can enjoy a design without wanting to wear it. That distinction is useful when the proposed gift is more dramatic than the person's ordinary clothes. Instead of interpreting a compliment as a specification, ask what they liked: the colour, the shape, the image, or the idea of wearing that particular kind of piece.

You could ask whether they would enjoy wearing something like it, or mainly enjoy looking at it. Keep the conversation relaxed. If they love the colour but would prefer a simpler shape, that gives you somewhere useful to begin. If they are enthusiastic about the garment itself, ask which outfit they picture it joining. The answer may reveal a neckline or length you would otherwise have guessed.

### Build a small brief

List what the recipient has actually said about wearing clothes. Perhaps they prefer an open front, avoid a particular sleeve length, or want a layer for an existing outfit. Leave blanks where you do not know. You can ask about one of those blanks without revealing the whole gift: a preferred sleeve, a useful length, or whether they want to try the garment themselves.

Check the practical details: relevant measurements, intended use, and whether the recipient wants to choose the version themselves. Read the seller's actual exchange or alteration terms. If there is a deadline or a condition attached to an exchange, put that information with the gift. A useful little note can save the recipient from having to ask you awkward questions after unwrapping it.

- A garment type the recipient wants to wear.
- A fit preference they have actually stated.
- A relevant measurement or a chance to try it.
- The practical options confirmed by the seller.

### Decide what should be a surprise

You can keep the occasion, wrapping, or final colour as a surprise while agreeing on the garment type and fit. You can also make choosing part of the gift: a visit to try several pieces, with time to consider them. Let the recipient decide whether that sounds enjoyable. Some people love the shared search; others would rather point to the exact piece they want.

If important information remains missing, pause the garment choice. Think about what you would want the recipient to do if it arrived and was not right. Be direct about their freedom to say so. A gift can be generous without asking someone to pretend it fits or enjoy a difficult fastening. Once the practical questions are answered, the pleasurable part of choosing has more room.

### Take this with you

Keep the surprise you value, but ask about the fit and wearing preferences you cannot safely infer.

### One question to keep

Which part of a clothing gift would you prefer to choose yourself?
