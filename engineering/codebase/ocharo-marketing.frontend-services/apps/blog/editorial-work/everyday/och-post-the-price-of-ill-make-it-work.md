# The price of 'I'll make it work'

*Last updated: 2026-09-27 12:07 AM*

## Editorial draft

Before accepting an almost-right piece, name every change it would ask of your wardrobe and your day.

**Reader promise:** Distinguish an enjoyable clothing project from a set of unresolved conditions attached to a purchase.

**Format:** Purchase reality check

## Manuscript

The bargain has a to-do list. Suppose an appealing garment needs shortening, a new underlayer, and a little patience with its fastening. Each task might be reasonable. Together, they describe a different purchase from the piece on the hanger. The thought of making it work becomes useful when the work has a name—and when you actually want to do it.

### What does the purchase ask you to do?

Complete the sentence: I would wear this if… Write each answer separately. If it needs an alteration, specify the change. If it needs another garment, name that garment. If it needs an occasion you rarely attend, say so. Include the small tasks too: finding a place for a tie, choosing different shoes, or keeping a sleeve away from your hands while working.

Suppose your list contains one familiar task and two guesses. A maker has assessed the sleeve you want to shorten, but you have not found the underlayer or tried wearing the fastening for more than a minute. Give each condition its own line. Then you can decide which questions to answer before buying, and which work you would be happy to take on afterwards.

Before deciding on an alteration, ask a maker to examine the construction and explain what is feasible. Show them the length or shape you want, and ask what else the change would affect. If a cuff or border is part of the piece's appeal, include it in the conversation. The solution should preserve the details that made you stop at the hanger in the first place.

For a missing companion piece, test something you already own. Try the neckline and length you had in mind, then look at the whole outfit. If the combination still feels unfinished, identify what is missing while you can still choose about the first garment. One new piece may open a much longer search. Decide whether that search sounds interesting to you or merely necessary.

- Confirmed: a change someone has assessed for this garment.
- Unresolved: a pairing, alteration, or use you still need to test.
- Chosen: work you expect to enjoy rather than postpone.

### Do you want that project?

Think about the sort of clothing task you enjoy. You might like finding combinations and revisiting a piece until it settles into use. Or you might want something that joins tomorrow's outfit with little attention. Imagine having the garment home, with the practical work still waiting. Which task would you begin gladly, and which would keep being moved to another day?

Ask which part attracts you most: the garment as it is, or the finished result you picture. If it is the result, work backwards. What must happen first? Who could answer the question you cannot answer yourself? A project can be a satisfying purchase when its tasks are known and wanted. The list helps you see the whole invitation before accepting it.

### Take this with you

List every 'if', confirm the feasible changes, and decide whether you want the resulting project.

### One question to keep

What clothing project have you enjoyed enough to choose again?
