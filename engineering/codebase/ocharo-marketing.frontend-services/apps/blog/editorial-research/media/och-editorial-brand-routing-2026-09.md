These are four reader-facing purposes, not a recommendation for four additional websites. Catalogue can be a top-level route to the existing product application. The user should not have to understand the app architecture.

## Engaging, professional formats to build

### 1. One textile, one revealing question

Open with a visible detail rather than a generic history paragraph. Examples of editorial questions: why an ikat outline appears feathered; how a repeated motif changes across a joined textile; what the reverse of an embroidery reveals. Each is a reporting brief, not a factual claim already established for a selected object.

Recommended feature rhythm:

1. One striking image and a question the reader can see.
2. A short opening scene tied to a documented object, place or real encounter.
3. An annotated image explaining one construction or pattern detail.
4. A compact place/time card showing documented context and uncertainty where relevant.
5. A making sequence or credited maker's voice, when reporting is available.
6. A contemporary connection: wearing, collecting, looking or making.
7. A related story and restrained brand signature.

Preserve the current readable layout; richer storytelling comes from the sequence of evidence, images and questions. A 500-word article with one reusable generated fashion image does not become a fabric atlas merely by changing its heading.

### 2. Pattern studies

Compare two or three specifically identified objects using matched crops. Highlight repeat, scale, border, colour arrangement or stitch direction. Keep observation separate from interpretation: “this shape resembles a…” is not equivalent to a documented regional motif name or symbolic meaning. Link each comparison back to the full object so cropping does not distort scale or use.

A professional series can ask readers to notice a difference without becoming a quiz about cultural authenticity. Avoid broad “how to spot a real Uzbek fabric” promises unless the article can establish the limits of its identification method.

### 3. Places and people

Create routes into the archive through **Bukhara, Samarqand, Margilan and other documented locations**. Region pages should show which objects, practices and makers the evidence connects to that place; they should not imply every motif belongs exclusively to one city. A map is navigation into stories, not decoration behind an unsourced history.

Maker visits, tools, workshop sounds, photo essays and short interviews can add original reporting. Begin with access already available; a research-supported museum object story can run before a travel commission. Attribute the maker, photographer, researcher and translator as relevant.

### 4. Dressing guides with a real decision

“One patterned layer, three settings” is useful when the photographs show what changes: base layer, coverage, footwear, bag or accessories. “What to wear in Samarqand” is too broad unless the guide names the activity, season and situation. Check venue-specific requirements when a religious or ceremonial setting is involved.

Professional tone comes from observable advice and workable alternatives. Include what to look for in a garment without requiring that garment to be Ocharo. Show an Ocharo product when it genuinely demonstrates the advice.

### 5. News and collection notes

Write news when there is a real new collection, event, collaboration, stockist or process development. Explain one meaningful change and its implications. A collection note can show why a cut, palette or placement changed if the designer has supplied that account. Do not invent weekly “behind the scenes” to satisfy a feed.

### 6. Product pages that support editorial

Keep catalogue facts maintained in their owning catalogue record. Editorial stories can link to a product or collection; they should not duplicate volatile prices or availability in several paragraphs. A sold-out item can remain in a relevant design story, with the product page carrying the current status.

## Routes into the main Ocharo channel

### Recommended reader journey

**Search, shared link or Instagram excerpt → specific useful article → related reading or relevant product → current Ocharo channel → conversation.**

The article should fulfil the original promise before offering a brand action. Leave a clear brand link in the header/footer so readers can act whenever they choose. Do not auto-redirect an article to Instagram or require a social follow to read it.

| Placement | Recommended behaviour | Why it belongs there |
|---|---|---|
| Site header | Small Ocharo/brand link, plus reading navigation | Makes authorship and destination available without interrupting reading |
| Story body | Contextual link only when the linked guide, maker or product explains the point | Preserves narrative flow |
| End of culture story | Related story as primary action; short Ocharo signature with optional brand link | A curious reader may want more culture before products |
| End of dressing guide | Relevant model/collection link when pictured; otherwise related guide | Matches the practical intent |
| Collection news | One collection or event action | The development itself justifies the action |
| Catalogue item | Specific inquiry action carrying the model identifier | Gives the buyer and operator shared context |
| Footer | Existing Instagram and Telegram destinations, accurately labelled | Consistent channel access across all surfaces |

A future **About Ocharo** page can explain the atelier and connect to catalogue and social profiles. Its route and domain remain implementation choices; no existing public home URL was verified here. Until there is a functioning owned destination, the recorded Instagram URL is the concrete brand route.

For social discovery, adapt one visual finding into a carousel or short clip and link to the full story through the account's available link surface. Do not promise a clickable Instagram feed-caption link. The exact posting mechanism belongs in the Marketing app's provider verification.

### Measurement without pretending clicks are sales

Record article identifier, vector, placement and destination for outbound actions. A link click is a link click; it does not prove a follow, Telegram subscription, private message or purchase. Use received inquiries naming a model/article, and subsequent qualified inquiries/orders, as separate downstream records. Avoid personal buyer details in URL parameters.

For the first pilot, compare useful reading actions, saves/shares where available, product visits, received inquiries and production time. The current static preview has no measurement baseline. These are proposed measures, not observed outcomes.

| Surface | Recommendation | Reason | Status |

| Creative garment tool | **Ocharo Studio** | Established creative-tool meaning and existing identity | Preserve current identity |
| Brand's operator tool | **Ocharo Marketing** | Explains the operational job; a poetic name adds little for operators | Recommended continuation |

“By Ocharo” is an endorsement, not a required suffix for every app. **Ocharo Blog by Ocharo** repeats the brand; **Ocharo Studio by Ocharo** has the same problem. Distinctive publication titles may benefit from the endorsement; functional tools generally benefit from clear names.

## Primary publication precedents

- [TOAST Magazine](https://www.toa.st/blogs/magazine), checked September 29, uses the parent brand plus a format label. It groups material/maker stories, style, travel and culture within the commerce site's navigation. The useful precedent is its relationship between publication and brand; the user's preference for a more contemporary layout remains unchanged.
- [Kvadrat's account of Daily Flower Report and Interwoven](https://www.kvadrat.dk/de/kunst-und-design/daily-flower-report-von-thomas-demand), checked September 29, identifies Interwoven as a platform for contemporary-culture stories supported by Kvadrat. It establishes that a textile brand can support a distinct editorial identity. This is a historical naming/ownership precedent, not proof of a currently maintained site or superior commercial performance.

These examples support both naming structures. They do not settle which one converts better for Ocharo. For Ocharo's present mix, the direct brand name and a strong named series keep the reader's route simplest.

## Proposed initial package

Build one substantial Uzbekistan textile feature, one practical dressing guide, and a connected current catalogue record where the assets support it. Add a news post only for an actual development. This package tests all necessary relationships without manufacturing four stories from one weak source.

Suggested navigation: **Textile stories · Wear it · Ocharo news · Catalogue**. A richer textile section can add **Places · Fabrics and techniques · Patterns · Makers** beneath its landing page. Those are discovery facets inside the culture vector, not new top-level vectors.

For the first cultural feature, choose a single well-documented object or technique with usable images and a clear connection to Bukhara, Samarqand or another relevant Uzbek location. The companion textile research should select the exact subject. Preserve Ocharo Blog during this build; test the publication promise through content before commissioning a separate identity.
