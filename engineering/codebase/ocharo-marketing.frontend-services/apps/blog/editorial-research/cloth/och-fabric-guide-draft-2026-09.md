*Last updated: 2026-09-29 11:59 AM (GMT+5)*

## Status

Twelve draft examples for a future guide, saved as editorial research only. They illustrate reader-facing language; they are not an exhaustive taxonomy or a published feature. The broader [research inventory](och-uzbek-textile-taxonomy-2026-09.md) separates fabrics, techniques, embroidery and motifs.

## Adras

**Category:** Fabric\
**Geographical scope:** Bukhara examples

Silk and cotton meet in historical adras: silk along the length, cotton across it.

**Look closer:** Look for the relationship between the flowing design and the small ribs of the surface. The State Museum account describes how a thicker cotton weft contributes that texture.

[Read the museum collection account](https://legacy.uz/en/what-fabric-samples-are-kept-in-the-collection-of-the-state-museum-of-arts-of-uzbekistan/).

## Atlas / xonatlas

**Category:** Fabric\
**Geographical scope:** Uzbekistan

Related silk-cloth names in Uzbekistan’s textile vocabulary, also written khan-atlas.

**Look closer:** Begin with a particular swatch: turn it towards the light, follow a fold, and compare its surface with adras. The Samarkand collection account lists both names among historical silk fabrics.

[Explore the Samarkand collection account](https://society.uz/news/detail/news/472).

## Bakhmal / alo-bakhmal

**Category:** Fabric\
**Geographical scope:** Bukhara examples

Velvet gives colour a raised surface. Bukhara examples include velvet ikats known as alo-bakhmal.

**Look closer:** Compare the impression of the velvet surface with a flat weave. What happens to a patterned area as it turns away from the light?

[Read the museum collection account](https://legacy.uz/en/what-fabric-samples-are-kept-in-the-collection-of-the-state-museum-of-arts-of-uzbekistan/).

## Bekasam / bekasab

**Category:** Fabric\
**Geographical scope:** Uzbekistan

A striped cloth whose coloured lengthwise threads create its rhythm.

**Look closer:** Follow one stripe from end to end. Compare the space between neighbouring bands before looking at the colours themselves.

[Read the museum collection account](https://legacy.uz/en/what-fabric-samples-are-kept-in-the-collection-of-the-state-museum-of-arts-of-uzbekistan/).

## Shoyi / shoi

**Category:** Fabric\
**Geographical scope:** Margilan examples

A silk-cloth name; museum holdings include Margilan examples from the early twentieth century.

**Look closer:** Let the surface catch your attention alongside the pattern. Compare a flat area with a fold and notice which details the light reveals.

[Read the museum collection account](https://legacy.uz/en/what-fabric-samples-are-kept-in-the-collection-of-the-state-museum-of-arts-of-uzbekistan/).

## Chit

**Category:** Fabric\
**Geographical scope:** Uzbekistan · Tashkent research

Cotton cloth connected to a rich history of printing, from workshops to factories.

**Look closer:** Find a repeat and follow where it returns. The Tashkent textile study shows how print designs also drew on embroidery and architectural decoration.

[Read San’at’s textile study](https://sanat.orexca.com/2012/2012-4/the-fabrics-of-the-tashkent-textile-factory-the-collection-of-the-state-fine-arts-museum-of-uzbekistan/).

## Abrbandi / warp ikat

**Category:** Technique\
**Geographical scope:** Bukhara example

A pattern begins in threads: selected areas are bound against dye before weaving.

**Look closer:** Follow a colour boundary in the Bukhara hanging. Imagine those lengthwise threads together before any crosswise thread entered the picture.

[See the Bukhara hanging](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2006.152).

## Suzani / so‘zana

**Category:** Embroidery\
**Geographical scope:** Bukhara example

An embroidered textile. A Bukhara-attributed cover at the Met opens a story of drawing, stitching and joining.

**Look closer:** Compare the decorated border with the more open centre. Follow a stem towards a seam and notice how the composition continues.

[See the Bukhara suzani](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/447384).

## Zarduzi / zardo‘zlik

**Category:** Embroidery\
**Geographical scope:** Bukhara

Gold embroidery has a long Bukhara history on clothing and accessories, including work on velvet.

**Look closer:** Follow the raised decoration across the ground. Look at its shape, spacing and changing reflection as separate parts of the composition.

[Read the Bukhara goldwork account](https://society.uz/news/detail/news/487).

## Chevrons and amulet forms

**Category:** Pattern\
**Geographical scope:** Bukhara example

The museum identifies different pattern families across the five widths of one Bukhara hanging.

**Look closer:** Find the two inner lengths with repeated chevrons, then compare their direction with the forms beside them. Return to the whole hanging to see how they work together.

[See the Bukhara hanging](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2006.152).

## Rosettes and dark curls

**Category:** Pattern\
**Geographical scope:** Samarkand examples

Large dark-red rosettes and dark curling forms organise several recorded Samarkand suzanis.

**Look closer:** Compare the size of a rosette with the whole cloth. How much space does one motif need before another begins?

[Explore the Samarkand suzani account](https://society.uz/news/detail/news/670).

## Borders, stems and palmettes

**Category:** Pattern\
**Geographical scope:** Bukhara example

A Bukhara-attributed suzani places bold border forms around a more delicate field of stems.

**Look closer:** Read the border as a frame, then follow one stem through the centre. How does the pace of the pattern change?

[See the Bukhara suzani](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/447384).
